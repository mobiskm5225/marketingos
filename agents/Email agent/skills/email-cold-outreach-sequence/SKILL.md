# Cold Outreach / Sales Sequence — Multi-Touch Framework

Source of truth: [Cold Outreach / Sales Sequence — Multi-Touch Framework](https://app.notion.com/p/3cb892c0700f81cc808dd460b965d649) in Reachify's Email Templates database (Format Key: `cold_outreach_sales_sequence`). If this file and the Notion page disagree, Notion wins — re-export this file.

## When to use

Use this shell for outbound prospecting sequences to cold B2B leads — no prior relationship or inbound signal. Not for nurturing an existing trial/signup user (use `email-onboarding-nurture-sequence`) and not for a recurring subscriber list (use `email-newsletter`). Core sequence is 4 emails; add the optional 5th "bump" email before the breakup when the ICP typically needs an extra nudge (e.g. longer sales cycles, enterprise buyers).

## Before drafting, gather

- Target ICP/persona definition (role, company size, industry)
- Personalization data source (what specific detail gets referenced per prospect — do not use generic compliments)
- Core value proposition / pain point angle for Email 1
- Case study or metric to cite in Email 2 — must be real and verifiable, never invented
- Resource/soft offer for Email 3 (asset name + link)
- Decision: include the optional bump email? (yes for long/enterprise cycles)
- Breakup email urgency framing (must be honest, not manufactured scarcity)
- Cadence (days between sends) and total sequence length
- Primary CTA type: reply vs. calendar link, and which tool/link to use
- Client's brand voice/positioning (pull from the client's Brand KB if one exists)

If any of these are missing, ask before drafting rather than inventing client-specific facts or fabricating a case study/metric.

## Structure

1. **Email 1 — Opening / Value Prop.** Lead with a personalized observation about the prospect or their company (not a generic compliment). Introduce yourself in one line. State the specific benefit relevant to their likely problem. Keep it short — 3–5 sentences total. End with a low-friction, curiosity-based question rather than a hard ask.
2. **Email 2 — Social Proof.** Reference the previous email briefly (one clause). Share one relevant case study or quantified result from a similar company/industry — specific numbers, not "great results." Keep concise; this email exists to build credibility, not to re-pitch.
3. **Email 3 — Resource / Soft Offer.** Offer something low-commitment and genuinely useful — a guide, benchmark data, a free trial/sample, a relevant tool. Frame it as removing friction to "try before you commit," not as a favour. Clear, simple next step to claim the resource.
4. **Email 4 (optional bump, for longer/enterprise cycles) — Re-surface.** Short, single-paragraph nudge referencing the resource/offer from Email 3; adds no new pitch content, just re-raises visibility.
5. **Final Email — Breakup / Final Ask.** Acknowledge the silence directly and without guilt-tripping. Recap the core benefit in one line. Create mild, honest urgency (e.g. "I'll stop reaching out after this") rather than manufactured scarcity. End with one clear, easy final action (reply or one-click scheduling link).

## Copy rules

- Personalize with real specifics — merge fields for name/company/title are the floor, not the ceiling; reference an actual detail about the company or a recent action.
- Keep every email short and to the point — this is a skim-read format, not a pitch deck in prose.
- Tone is conversational and helpful, never pushy; every draft should pass "would I reply to this from a stranger?"
- CTA softens in directness across the sequence — curiosity question → soft resource ask → direct calendar/reply ask by the breakup email.
- Space sends roughly 3–7 days apart; never send two emails back-to-back with no gap.
- Every claim in the social-proof email must be a real, checkable result — never invent a statistic.

## Output format

Return, per email in the sequence: subject line (2–3 options), full body copy, and the CTA. Note the chosen cadence (days between sends) and whether the optional bump email was included.
