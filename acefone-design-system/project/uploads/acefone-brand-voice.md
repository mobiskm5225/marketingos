## 1. The Foundation

### The Brand Voice Name: Trusted Clarity

Acefone sounds like a senior operator who has seen the inside of a contact center, knows what actually breaks, and can explain exactly how to fix it — without jargon, without hype, and without wasting anyone's time. Warm enough that a CX manager trusts us. Authoritative enough that a VP takes us to their board.

### The Archetype: Empathetic Partner + Intelligent Simplifier

We lead with understanding. We know our reader's day — the back-to-back calls, the agent attrition meeting, the CRM that doesn't talk to the phone system. We acknowledge that world before offering a way out of it. And when we explain the solution, we make the complex tangible. We don't hide behind features. We translate everything into decisions and outcomes.

### The Reference Point: HubSpot × Salesforce

HubSpot's warmth — educational, helpful, never talking down to the reader. Salesforce's authority — data-backed, enterprise-grade, earns the right to be in the room. Acefone sits at the intersection: the trusted advisor who is genuinely on your side. Not a vendor. Not a consultant. A partner who makes operations better.

### The Voice Spectrum

| Dimension | Never sounds like | Always sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| Register | Cold, feature-dumping, jargon-heavy | Warm, outcome-led, human |
| Credibility | All promise, no proof, buzzword-heavy | Every claim backed by data or specifics |
| Pace | Slow, stiff, legacy-telco formality | Direct, modern, moves with purpose |
| Pressure | Every line is a pitch, conversion-obsessed | Useful first, commercial second |
| Audience | Speaks to a generic "business" | Speaks to a specific person with a real job |

### Two Feelings We Always Leave Behind

**1. Practical Relief**
"This will actually make my life easier." The reader feels like they've found a solution that fits their real operations — not a tool they'll spend 6 months configuring.

**2. Operational Confidence**
"I can trust this company with our systems." The tone signals stability, depth, and genuine expertise — not a startup that might pivot tomorrow.

### Four Things We Never Sound Like

- ✗ **Generic IT vendor** — cold, feature-first, no human in the room
- ✗ **Overhyped startup** — vague superlatives, no data, too much "game-changing"
- ✗ **Legacy telco** — formal to the point of stiffness, slow, behind the curve
- ✗ **Pushy sales machine** — every sentence moves toward the close

## 2. The Four Voice Pillars

Every piece of Acefone content — a LinkedIn post, a homepage headline, a sales deck slide, an email subject line — should express at least one of these four pillars. Together, they define what "Trusted Clarity" actually means in writing.

---

### Pillar 01 — Grounded Clarity
*"We make the complex simple. We don't dumb it down."*

Our readers are smart operators who deal with complex systems daily. We respect that by being direct and clear — not by over-explaining or hiding behind technical language. If we need three sentences, we use three. Not eight.

**Voice sample:**
> "Your agents aren't slow. They're spending 60% of their time answering questions a bot should handle. That's not a people problem. That's a process problem."

---

### Pillar 02 — Earned Authority
*"We earn the right to be believed. We don't just claim it."*

We speak with confidence, but we back every claim with specifics — data, outcomes, customer results, industry benchmarks. Vague superlatives are not authority. Specific numbers are. "Up to 70% cost reduction" is a claim. "2.5 billion engagements annually" is a credential.

**Voice sample:**
> "AI calls cost between $0.50 and $5. Human agent calls cost between $5 and $25. For a contact center handling one million calls a year, that arithmetic matters."

---

### Pillar 03 — Operational Empathy
*"We see your world before we offer ours."*

Before we introduce the solution, we name the problem — specifically, in the language our readers use internally. A Head of Support doesn't think in "call deflection rates." They think in "my team is answering the same ten questions all day." We start there.

**Voice sample:**
> "You've invested in good people. But peak season hits, call volume doubles, and suddenly you're asking your best agents to handle tasks a bot could manage in seconds. That's the gap Acefone closes."

---

### Pillar 04 — Decisive Optimism
*"Things can be better. Here's exactly how."*

We never wallow in the problem. We acknowledge it — then we move forward. Acefone content should always point toward a better operational reality. Not with empty positivity, but with a clear, believable path. Every pain point we name has a corresponding solution we own.

**Voice sample:**
> "Most contact centers don't have a people problem. They have a system problem. When the system works — routing is intelligent, bots handle Tier 1, agents see the full customer picture — the team performs."

## 3. 10 Writing Rules

### Rule 01 — Lead with the problem, not the product

The first sentence of any Acefone content should reflect something true about the reader's world — not about Acefone. Earn the right to introduce the solution by first acknowledging the pain.

- **Not this:** "Acefone AI Voice Bot is an intelligent automation solution designed to handle repetitive customer queries around the clock."
- **This:** "Your agents are talented. They shouldn't spend their day answering the same ten questions on repeat. Acefone AI Voice Bot handles those — so they don't have to."

---

### Rule 02 — Use "you" and "your team" more than "we" and "our"

Acefone content should feel like it's about the reader, not about Acefone. Every piece should pass this test: does this sentence serve the reader or congratulate ourselves? The ratio should be roughly 3:1 — you:we.

- **Not this:** "We built Acefone to help businesses modernise their communication infrastructure with cutting-edge cloud technology."
- **This:** "Your contact center doesn't need more software. It needs the right infrastructure — one that routes smarter, automates the routine, and gives your team time back."

---

### Rule 03 — Translate features into outcomes — always

Never list a feature without its business result. "AI Voice Bot" is a feature. "Handle 60% of inbound calls without adding headcount" is an outcome. Always pair them. Features go in parentheses; outcomes go in headlines.

- **Not this:** "Real-time dashboards, call recording, IVR configuration, and CRM integrations."
- **This:** "See what's happening across every queue, catch issues before they escalate, and keep your CRM in sync — without manual data entry."

---

### Rule 04 — Use short sentences as anchors

Short sentences carry the weight. They create rhythm and emphasis. Use them to land key ideas, then use longer sentences to add context, nuance, or proof. Vary deliberately.

- **Good rhythm:** "Scaling shouldn't mean hiring. Acefone AI Voice Bot handles the volume your team can't — without overtime costs, without quality trade-offs, and without waiting weeks for onboarding."

---

### Rule 05 — Stats earn the right to make claims

Every strong claim must be earned by a number. Not "significant cost savings" — "up to 70% reduction in operational costs." Not "faster resolution" — "from hours to minutes." When you have a stat, lead with it. When you don't, don't make the claim.

- **Not this:** "Acefone helps businesses dramatically reduce costs and improve customer satisfaction significantly."
- **This:** "Contact centres using AI voice automation report up to 70% reduction in cost per call and a 15–25% improvement in CSAT. The numbers are consistent across industries."

---

### Rule 06 — Use definitive language — never hedge without reason

Avoid "we try to," "we hope," "we aim to," "we believe." These signal uncertainty and erode trust. Acefone content speaks from a position of capability and confidence.

- **Not this:** "We believe our platform can help teams improve efficiency and hopefully reduce costs over time."
- **This:** "Acefone cuts Tier 1 call volume by up to 60%, typically within the first 90 days of deployment."

---

### Rule 07 — Name the specific role, not "businesses"

Our primary readers are CX/Contact Center leaders and IT/Ops managers. Content should speak to them as individuals with specific jobs — not abstract "organisations" or "companies."

- **Not this:** "Businesses looking to modernise their communication infrastructure can benefit from Acefone."
- **This:** "If you're running a contact centre of 50+ agents, you know the staffing equation doesn't get easier. Acefone changes what that equation looks like."

---

### Rule 08 — End sections with what comes next

Every section — a landing page block, a LinkedIn post, a slide — should have a natural forward direction. Either a CTA, a question that prompts reflection, or a bridge to the next idea.

- **Good close:** "The teams seeing the fastest ROI aren't the ones with the biggest budgets — they're the ones who deployed first. [Book a demo to see the setup timeline →]"

---

### Rule 09 — Write active voice, always

Passive voice is evasive and corporate. Active voice is direct and signals confidence. There is almost no valid reason to use passive voice in Acefone content.

- **Not this:** "Calls can be automatically routed to the right agent based on skills and availability."
- **This:** "Acefone routes every call to the right agent — based on skill, availability, and customer history — automatically."

---

### Rule 10 — One idea per sentence. One theme per paragraph.

A paragraph that tries to cover cost savings, integration capability, and deployment speed will land none of them. Isolate ideas. Give each one room.

- **Not this:** "Acefone AI Voice Bot reduces operational costs while also providing 24/7 availability and integrating seamlessly with your CRM and helpdesk tools, which means your team can handle more volume."
- **This:** "Cost per call drops by up to 70%. Your team is available 24/7 — without the overtime bill. And every conversation syncs automatically to Salesforce, HubSpot, or Zendesk. No manual logging."

## 4. Channel Adaptations

The voice stays consistent. The calibration shifts. Think of it as the same person — same values, same knowledge — adjusting their register for the room they're in.

---

### LinkedIn — Organic Posts
*Awareness · Thought Leadership*

| Dimension | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Register | Most conversational of the three channels. Still professional — this is a CX VP's feed, not a consumer app. Natural, direct, human-feeling. First person plural ("we") is allowed but keep the balance toward "you." |
| Hook structure | Always open with a single punchy statement that names a real operational truth. Not a question. Not a listicle intro. A statement that makes the reader stop. Then expand. |
| Length | 150–250 words. Short paragraphs of 1–2 sentences. Line breaks are intentional — use them for rhythm. Never write a LinkedIn post that looks like a paragraph from a whitepaper. |
| Claims & data | Use one strong stat per post. Make it the anchor. Don't stack five numbers — it looks like a data dump. One stat, well-framed, lands harder than five stats poorly used. |
| Close | Soft CTA ("If this sounds familiar, here's what the fix looks like → [link]") or a reflective statement that positions Acefone as a thought partner, not a vendor. Avoid hard-sell closes on organic posts. |

---

### LinkedIn — Paid / Sponsored
*Lead Generation · Demo Requests*

| Dimension | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Register | Tighter and more direct than organic. Paid has 3 seconds. The first line must be a provocation or a specific, surprising number. Slightly more commercial — a CTA is expected. |
| Formula | Lead with the pain or the stat → one-sentence product pivot → specific proof point or outcome → single CTA. The ad's job is one click. |
| Headlines | Short, specific, provocative. "Your agents cost $5–$25 per call. AI costs $0.50–$5." "80% of businesses will use voice AI by 2026." "Handle 10x your call volume. Without hiring 10x your team." |
| CTA language | Use action + specificity: "Book a Demo" beats "Learn More." "Calculate Your Savings" beats "Get Started." Always name the value of the click. |

---

### Website — Landing Pages & Product Pages
*Consideration · Conversion*

| Dimension | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Register | Authoritative and clear. The website is where authority lives. The tone should convey depth and reliability without becoming formal or stiff. |
| Hero section | Lead with an outcome headline, not a product description. "Cut contact center costs by 70%" or "Your customers shouldn't wait." The subheadline introduces the product in one clean sentence. |
| Body copy | Outcome-first benefit headers. Each section header should be a result: "Resolve queries in seconds, not minutes" not "Fast response times." Structure: benefit headline → how it works (1 sentence) → proof (1 stat or outcome). |
| Stats | Use stat callouts generously — they are credibility anchors. The numbers that work hardest: 70% cost reduction, 90%+ first-contact resolution, ROI in 3–6 months, 2.5 billion engagements annually. |
| CTAs | Primary CTA on every page above the fold. "Book a Demo" is the primary conversion goal. Secondary CTAs serve consideration-stage visitors. Never have a page without both. |

---

### Sales Decks & Proposals
*Evaluation · Close*

| Dimension | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Register | Most authoritative and data-dense of all channels. The reader is in evaluation mode — comparing options, building a business case. Write as if you're in the room with them. |
| Slide copy | Headlines should be conclusions, not titles. Not "Cost Analysis" — "AI voice automation reduces your cost per call by up to 70%." Body copy should contain only what the speaker wouldn't say — the stat, the proof point. |
| Persona matching | CX leader: lead with CSAT improvement, agent productivity, FCR. IT/Ops: lead with deployment timeline, integration reliability, security. CFO: lead with cost-per-call math, payback period, 3-year ROI. |
| Differentiation | Be specific about why not the alternatives. "Unlike standalone bots, Acefone is built into a complete platform — so AI Voice Bot, Contact Center Studio, and Post Conversation Analytics work together." Name the gap competitors leave. |

## 5. Persona Voice Guide

Same brand voice. Shifted emphasis. The two primary audiences share a reader profile but differ in what they need to feel confident about Acefone.

---

### Persona 1 — CX / Contact Center Leader
*VP Customer Experience · Head of Support · CX Manager · Support Team Lead*

**Their world:**
Managing agent performance, handling peak volume spikes, watching CSAT scores, fighting high attrition, justifying headcount to finance, and being held accountable when the queue blows up. They care about operations, outcomes, and their team.

**What they need to feel:**
"This company understands how a contact center actually works. They're not selling me a product — they're solving a problem I have every day." Practical relief, validated by someone who gets their world.

**Lead with:** Operational outcomes first: handle time, first-contact resolution, call abandonment rate, agent productivity. Make the day easier before making the business case.

**Stats to use:**
- 90%+ first-contact resolution
- 70% cost reduction
- 40% fewer abandoned calls
- 15% reduction in average handle time
- 13.8% more conversations per agent per hour with AI assist
- CSAT improves 15–25% post-deployment

**Language to use:** "Your team," "your agents," "resolve," "handle," "queue," "first-contact," "peak volume," "consistent service quality," "on-hold time," "agent workload"

**Avoid:** Heavy ROI/financial language in the opening. Don't lead with cost savings for this persona — lead with operational improvement. The cost savings follow once the operational case is made.

---

### Persona 2 — IT / Operations Leader
*CTO · IT Manager · VP Operations · Head of Sales Ops · Solutions Architect*

**Their world:**
Owning integration complexity, managing vendor relationships, evaluating build-vs-buy, ensuring uptime and reliability, dealing with security and compliance requirements, and trying to deploy things that actually work without 18-month timelines.

**What they need to feel:**
"This will work with what we already have, deploy fast, and not become my problem in six months." Confidence in stability, integration, and speed of value delivery.

**Lead with:** Integration simplicity, deployment timeline, uptime/reliability, security credentials (ISO/IEC 27001, AWS Advanced Tier), and the fact that the CX team can manage day-to-day without constant IT involvement.

**Stats to use:**
- Deploys in weeks, not 6–18 months
- Pre-built integrations: Salesforce, Zoho, HubSpot, Freshsales, Zendesk, Freshdesk, Microsoft Teams
- ISO/IEC 27001 certified · AWS Advanced Tier Partner
- 0.05s latency on Voice Streaming
- 150+ countries via Global VoIP

**Language to use:** "Pre-built," "deploy," "integrate," "configure," "reliable," "uptime," "secure," "API," "stack," "live in weeks," "no rip-and-replace"

**Avoid:** Vague words like "seamless" without context. IT leaders have seen "seamless integrations" fail. Be specific: name the systems, name the method, name the timeline. Don't promise easy — demonstrate it.

## 6. Product Voice Architecture

The Acefone master brand carries the "Trusted Clarity" voice. Individual products have their own voice calibration — same personality root, different register. Products are more task-focused, less emotive, more precise.

---

### Acefone Master Brand — *Trusted Clarity*
*Corporate · Umbrella Brand*

**Register:** Empathetic Partner + Intelligent Simplifier. Speaks to the whole organisation. Bridges the operational world of CX and the technical world of IT under one trusted platform identity.

**Sample line:**
> "2.5 billion customer interactions handled annually. Behind every one: the right technology, working quietly, so your team doesn't have to think about it."

---

### AI Voice Bot — *Efficient Precision*
*Product Voice*

**Register:** Sharper, more direct, and more commercially focused than master brand. Leads with data and speed. Less warmth, more decisiveness. The AI Voice Bot voice is about output: what gets handled, how fast, at what cost.

**Tone shift:** Master brand: "We understand your team's world." AI Voice Bot: "Here's exactly what the bot handles — and what it costs when it doesn't."

**Key words:** Automate · Handle · Resolve · Deflect · Scale · Cost per call · First-contact · Available 24/7

**Sample line:**
> "Every call the bot handles costs $0.50–$5. Every call your agent handles costs $5–$25. For one million calls a year, that's a $3–5 million decision."

---

### Interactions Hub — *Unified Visibility*
*Product Voice*

**Register:** Team-centred. Speaks to the operational leader who needs their agents to be productive and their customers to feel known. Moderate warmth, strong emphasis on control and clarity.

**Key words:** Unified · Omnichannel · Agent workspace · Real-time · Customer journey · One view · No context lost

**Sample line:**
> "Voice, chat, WhatsApp, email — your agents handle every channel from one workspace. Your customers never repeat themselves again."

---

### API Connect — *Developer Confidence*
*Product Voice*

**Register:** Most technical of all product voices. Speaks to developers and solutions architects. Precise, capability-led, low on metaphor. Trust is built through specificity of technical capability, not emotional resonance.

**Key words:** Programmable · API · Embed · Integrate · SDK · Scalable infrastructure · Webhook · Documentation

**Sample line:**
> "Embed voice calling, number masking, and 2FA into your application in under a day. API documentation that developers actually use."

---

### Xtract (Post Conversation Analytics) — *Intelligence Lens*
*Product Voice*

**Register:** Strategic and insight-forward. Speaks to senior CX and operations leaders who need to turn call data into decisions. Analytical but not dry — the insight is always anchored to an operational outcome.

**Key words:** Insight · Sentiment · Trends · 100% call analysis · Keyword spotting · Performance · Decisions · Pattern

**Sample line:**
> "100% of your calls, analysed. Not sampled. Xtract surfaces what's working, what's not, and where the next improvement is — before your next QA review."

## 7. Vocabulary Guide

### Use These Words — They're Distinctly Acefone

Resolve · Automate · Handle · Deflect · Route · Connect · Scale · Streamline · Unify · Intelligent · Real-time · Instant · Reliable · Proven · Consistent · Deploy in weeks · Your team · Operations · No rip-and-replace · Works with · First-contact · Cost per call · Agent productivity · Omnichannel

### Avoid These Words — They Signal the Wrong Voice

~~Leverage~~ · ~~Synergy~~ · ~~World-class~~ · ~~Best-in-class~~ · ~~Cutting-edge~~ · ~~Game-changing~~ · ~~Next-generation~~ · ~~Robust~~ · ~~Seamlessly~~ · ~~Revolutionize~~ · ~~Disrupting~~ · ~~Empower~~ · ~~Holistic~~ · ~~Ecosystem~~ · ~~At the end of the day~~ · ~~Significantly~~ · ~~Various~~ · ~~Utilize~~ · ~~Facilitate~~ · ~~Innovative solution~~ · ~~Going forward~~

> These aren't banned — they're signals. If you find yourself reaching for one, it usually means the sentence needs more specificity, not a different word.

### CTA Language Guide

| Stage | Use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | "See how it works →" / "Explore the platform →" / "Read the guide →" | "Learn More" / "Click Here" |
| Consideration | "Calculate your savings →" / "Compare AI vs IVR →" / "Watch a live demo call →" | "Get Started" / "Find Out More" |
| Conversion | "Book a Demo" / "Talk to an Expert" / "Get a Custom ROI Estimate" | "Submit" / "Contact Us" |
